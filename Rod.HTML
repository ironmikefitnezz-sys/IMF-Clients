Readiness Assessment
Your numbers look solid today Rod, and that mild leg soreness from Saturday is completely normal after a full body session so the plan today accounts for that and keeps you moving forward.
Warm-Up
5 to 7 minutes to get your chest and upper body primed and ready to work. Band Pull-Aparts: Stand tall and hold a resistance band in front of you at chest height with both hands. Pull the band apart by squeezing your shoulder blades together, then slowly return. Do 15 reps. This opens up your chest and gets your upper back ready to stabilize. Arm Circles: Extend both arms out to your sides and make controlled circles, 10 forward and 10 backward. Loosens up the shoulder joint before pressing. Incline Push-Ups on a bench: 2 sets of 10 at a slow pace. This is your active warm-up for the chest, gets blood moving through the muscles you are about to train without pre-fatiguing them.
Main Block
Today is Tuesday and that means chest. Your legs are a little sore from Saturday so today works perfectly since chest training puts zero demand on them. All metrics are strong so you are getting a full session today. Exercise 1: Flat Barbell Bench Press 4 sets of 8 reps. Set your back flat against the bench, plant your feet on the floor, and press the bar up and slightly back toward your face at the top. Lower it slowly to your chest over about 2 seconds, then press. Rest 75 seconds between sets. This is your primary chest builder and the anchor of today's session. Exercise 2: Incline Dumbbell Press 3 sets of 10 reps. Set the bench to a 30 to 45 degree angle. Press the dumbbells up from your shoulders until your arms are fully extended, then lower slowly. This targets the upper portion of your chest which adds the thickness and fullness that ties the whole muscle together. Rest 60 seconds between sets. Exercise 3: Cable Chest Fly (using a cable machine with handles set to chest height) 3 sets of 12 reps. Stand in the center of the cable station, grab both handles, and bring them together in front of your chest in a wide hugging motion. Hold for one second at the center and feel the squeeze, then slowly return. This isolates the chest without heavy joint loading and keeps the pump going. Rest 60 seconds between sets. Exercise 4: Chest Dip (using parallel bars, leaning slightly forward) 3 sets of 10 reps. Lean your torso slightly forward as you lower yourself between the bars, then press back up. Keeping that forward lean is what keeps the focus on your chest rather than your triceps. If bodyweight feels too easy, add a dumbbell between your legs or use a weight belt. Rest 60 seconds between sets. Exercise 5: Dumbbell Pullover 2 sets of 12 reps. Lie flat on a bench and hold one dumbbell with both hands above your chest. Lower it slowly behind your head in a wide arc, feeling a stretch through your chest, then pull it back up. This stretches the chest under load which supports muscle growth. Rest 60 seconds between sets.
Cool-Down
5 minutes to bring your chest and shoulders down easy and reduce next-day stiffness. Doorway or Cable Chest Stretch: Step into a doorway or use a cable tower, place your forearm against the frame at shoulder height, and gently rotate your body away until you feel a stretch across your chest. Hold 30 seconds each side. Cross-Body Shoulder Stretch: Pull one arm across your chest and hold it with your opposite hand. Hold 20 to 30 seconds each side. Your front shoulder took some load today and this keeps it feeling open. Child's Pose with Arms Extended Forward: Kneel on the floor, sit your hips back toward your heels, and reach both arms out in front of you on the ground. Hold for 30 to 45 seconds. This gently decompresses your spine after all that pressing and lets your whole upper body relax.
Coach Note
Chest day fell on the right day for you Rod because your legs get a full pass today, and with your energy where it is you are in a great position to push your pressing numbers and put some real work into building that upper chest thickness you need to stay lean and balanced at your size.
