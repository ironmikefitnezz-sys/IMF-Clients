Readiness Assessment
Rod, you are locked in today — sleep was perfect, energy is maxed out, no soreness anywhere, and stress is basically nonexistent, so you are walking into this chest session with everything you need to push forward.
Warm-Up
5 minutes to get your chest, shoulders, and elbows ready to work. Band Pull-Aparts or Arm Circles (2 minutes) — stand tall and pull a light band or swing your arms in wide circles to open up the shoulders and get blood moving into the joints you are about to load. Push-Up to Down Dog (90 seconds) — do a slow push-up, then press your hips up and back into an inverted V position, hold for a breath, then return. This warms up the chest and shoulders together while loosening the upper back. Light Dumbbell Chest Press, 1 set of 15 reps with very light weight (90 seconds) — this is not a working set, just a rehearsal. Focus on feeling the chest stretch at the bottom and contract at the top.
Main Block
Today is a chest day and your readiness is as good as it gets, so the goal is to push load or volume beyond what you hit in your last comparable chest session. Full effort, full rest, and full focus on the muscle doing the work. Barbell Bench Press 4 sets of 6 reps, rest 90 seconds between sets This is your primary strength movement for the day. Load this heavier than your last chest session if your form held up well. Lower the bar with control to your mid-chest and drive it up powerfully. Do not let your elbows flare straight out to the sides — keep them at roughly a 45 degree angle from your body. Incline Dumbbell Press 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 75 seconds between sets Set the bench to about 30 to 45 degrees. This targets the upper portion of your chest which is key for a well-rounded, full look. Control the descent so you feel a stretch at the bottom, then press up and slightly inward at the top. Cable Chest Fly (using the cable machine with handles set at chest height) 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 seconds between sets Keep a slight bend in your elbows throughout the entire movement. Think about hugging a large tree. This isolates the chest and keeps constant tension on the muscle through the full range of motion, which is something a dumbbell fly or barbell cannot do. Chest Dip (parallel bar dips leaning slightly forward) 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, rest 60 seconds between sets Lean your torso slightly forward as you dip to shift the emphasis from your triceps onto your lower chest. Lower yourself until your upper arms are roughly parallel to the floor, then press back up. If bodyweight becomes too easy, add weight using a dip belt. High-to-Low Cable Fly (cable handles set above head height, pulling down and across) 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 seconds between sets This angle targets the lower chest and adds definition to the bottom edge of the muscle. Keep the movement controlled and squeeze at the bottom of each rep where your hands meet.
Cool-Down
5 minutes to bring everything back down and keep your chest and shoulders healthy for the week ahead. Doorway or Cable Machine Chest Stretch (90 seconds) — place your forearm against a wall or cable post and gently rotate your body away until you feel a deep stretch across your chest. Hold for 30 to 45 seconds on each side. Cross-Body Shoulder Stretch (60 seconds) — pull one arm across your chest and hold for 20 to 30 seconds per side. This relieves tension in the rear shoulder that builds up during pressing movements. Cat-Cow or Thoracic Extension Over a Bench (90 seconds) — sit on a bench and place your hands behind your head, then gently extend your upper back over the edge of the bench. This opens up the thoracic spine which gets compressed during heavy pressing and keeps your posture from rounding over time.
Coach Note
You have a full chest session in front of you with a green light across the board, Rod, so the only thing left to do is make sure you are actually challenging yourself on that bench press — if the weight you used last time felt like you had two or three reps left in the tank, today is the day to go heavier and keep building toward that physique you are working for.
