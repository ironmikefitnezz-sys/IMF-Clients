Readiness Assessment
Your numbers look solid today Rod, and that light leg soreness from Saturday is completely normal after a full body session, so we will work around it and put today's chest day to good use.
Warm-Up
5 minutes to get the upper body primed and ready. Arm circles forward and backward, 10 reps each direction, to loosen up the shoulders before any pressing. Band pull-aparts or lightweight rear delt flys, 15 reps slow and controlled, to activate the muscles that stabilize your shoulder during pressing movements. Push-up to downward dog, 8 reps moving smoothly through each position, to open the chest and warm up the pressing pattern with no load. Light dumbbell chest press, 15 reps at about 40 percent of your working weight, just to get blood into the muscle and rehearse the movement before the real work begins.
Main Block
Today is chest day and all metrics are in a good place, so this is a full session. Legs are a little sore from Saturday but chest training puts zero demand on them, so there is no conflict here. We are building muscle, which means controlled reps, full range of motion, and pushing close to your limit on each set without sacrificing form. Flat Barbell Bench Press 4 sets of 8 reps, rest 75 seconds between sets This is your primary strength builder for the session. Lower the bar to your chest with control, about 2 seconds on the way down, then press it up with intent. On your last set, push as close to failure as you safely can with a spotter or safety bars in place. Incline Dumbbell Press 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 60 seconds between sets Set the bench to about 30 to 45 degrees. This targets the upper portion of the chest, which is key for a full, developed look. Lower the dumbbells until your elbows are just below shoulder level, then press them up and together at the top. Cable Chest Fly (using the cable machine set at mid-height) 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 seconds between sets Keep a slight bend in your elbows throughout and focus on squeezing the chest as you bring your hands together in front of you. This is an isolation movement, so leave the ego out of the weight selection and focus on feeling the stretch and contraction. Dumbbell Chest Press on Flat Bench 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 seconds between sets This is higher rep work to finish the chest with volume. Keep the movement controlled and squeeze at the top of each rep. If the weight feels easy by rep 12 you can go up next week. Dip (chest variation, leaning slightly forward) 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 60 seconds between sets Lean your torso slightly forward as you dip to shift the emphasis onto the chest rather than the triceps. Lower yourself until your elbows reach about 90 degrees, then press back up. If bodyweight feels too easy, add a dumbbell between your legs using a dip belt.
Cool-Down
5 minutes to bring the chest down from the work and keep the muscles long. Doorway or cable chest stretch, hold for 30 seconds each side, to open up the pec muscles while they are still warm. Cross-body shoulder stretch, 30 seconds each arm, to release tension in the rear shoulder that gets recruited during pressing. Overhead tricep stretch, 20 seconds each arm, since the triceps assist on every pressing movement today and will benefit from a light stretch. Deep diaphragmatic breathing, 5 slow breaths in through the nose and out through the mouth, to bring the nervous system down and signal recovery.
Coach Note
Chest sessions are where a lot of your upper body size is going to come from Rod, so the flat bench and incline work today are the most important sets to protect. Keep the reps clean, the range of motion full, and track your weights so next week you have a number to beat.
